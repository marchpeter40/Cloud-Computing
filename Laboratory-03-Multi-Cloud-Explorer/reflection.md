# Mission Reflection

Completing Mission 3 as a Multi-Cloud Explorer has significantly deepened my understanding of the three leading public cloud platforms. Among AWS, Microsoft Azure, and Google Cloud Platform, the platform that impressed me the most is Google Cloud Platform. Its clean developer experience, industry-leading Kubernetes support through GKE, and powerful AI/ML tooling such as Vertex AI and BigQuery stood out. The fact that the same infrastructure powers Google’s own global products gives me confidence in its performance and reliability.

Despite their competitive differences, AWS, Azure, and GCP share many fundamental similarities. All three follow a pay-as-you-go pricing model, offer virtual machines, object storage, managed databases, identity and access management, and virtual networking. They each provide global infrastructure with multiple regions and availability zones, strong security and compliance programs, and support for hybrid and multi-cloud strategies. The core building blocks of cloud computing—compute, storage, networking, and identity—are present and mature on every platform.

Business requirements are the primary driver of cloud platform selection. A startup with limited budget and rapid growth expectations benefits from AWS’s free tier and vast service catalog. An organization already invested in Microsoft 365 and Active Directory naturally gravitates toward Azure to minimize disruption and maximize existing license value. AI research teams prioritize GCP for its specialized hardware and software stack. Global e-commerce platforms often choose AWS for its extensive geographic footprint and battle-tested scaling capabilities. Matching technical strengths to business context is the essence of good cloud architecture.

Acting as a Cloud Solutions Consultant taught me that technical excellence alone is not enough. Recommendations must be grounded in the client’s existing technology investments, skill sets, regulatory constraints, and growth trajectory. I learned to evaluate trade-offs systematically rather than defaulting to the most popular provider. This mindset of “choose the platform that best solves the problem” is foundational for any future cloud architect or engineer.

Finally, my GitHub Cloud Computing Portfolio has become more professional and organized after this mission. The addition of Laboratory-03-Multi-Cloud-Explorer with structured research documents, comparison tables, client recommendations, and evidence screenshots demonstrates continuous learning and the ability to produce clear technical documentation. Maintaining consistent commit history and a clean repository structure prepares me for real-world collaboration and future portfolio reviews.
